## Problem

Please do some research on :
1. How the secure coding course can be mandated by the team's director or above person rather than by QPSI. 
2. Since large portions of code that we expect to be autogenerated with secure coding guardrails, training requirements have shifted.
3. we feel we can loosen the tight grip on this and let the team decide what they want. 
4. We are putting in place a policy where leads will be able to decide if the training is necessary for their team and what kind of controls they want to exercise. That means the QPSI mandated auto-blocklisting developers write permissions to GIT/Perforce will now be a knob that leads will have control over.

## Current status

Common query we get:
1. Recently completed a the training but they still don't have permission.
2. They have crossed the due date and their access is revoked and blacklisted.
3. They have not completed all the trainings.

Most of the training related work we do is just fire fighting with the list and channels team. We can shift that to their respective orgs and some on their team will do that instead. It's possible but the work is mostly operational in nature.

Earlier we use to debug the manually and deal with the issue but now we have create a sample template email which show how to deal with each of the above scenarios to the candidate and we are able to handle is without much work and more gracefully.

## Solutions

1. We will put up a short training explaining the different scenarios people get blocked, our recommendations for situations, etc.
2. This training will be very similar to Klockwork, we will show what different scenarios and how to deal/fix the scenarios.
3. We can add leads from each of the team as group admins for the groups we are managing and they can add and remove people from those groups, they will be their call. If they need assistance we will help them. This way we are delegating the control to the respective teams and still be around for further assistance.
